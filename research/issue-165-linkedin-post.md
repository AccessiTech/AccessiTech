### Final issue reply

**Hook**  
Open source is part of accessibility.

**Bulk**  
That is especially true in web accessibility, where so much progress depends on shared standards, community-built tooling, and knowledge that anyone can learn from, adapt, and improve.

That is why Nvidia's acquisition of Hugging Face feels important beyond AI headlines. There is real potential here: more investment, more reach, and more momentum for open tooling. But there is also real risk. If access to the ecosystem becomes more dependent on specific chips, closed infrastructure, or vendor-controlled optimization paths, then "open" starts to mean something much narrower in practice.

And when that happens, accessibility takes a hit.

Open systems matter because they lower barriers to entry. They make it easier for smaller teams, independent developers, and disabled technologists to participate without needing the most expensive hardware or the most privileged position in the stack.

This is part of why the GitHub/Microsoft comparison comes to mind for me. Sometimes acquisition brings scale and durability. Sometimes it also concentrates power over the very commons people depend on.

I'm curious how others are reading this: is this a net gain for open source, or a warning sign that accessibility and openness are getting harder to separate from compute power?
